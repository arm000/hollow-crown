# Setting & Story

> "Within the hollow crown / that rounds the mortal temples of a king /
> keeps Death his court." — the title is a borrowed line, and the game
> means to earn it.

## The premise

The kingdom of **Ashveil** fell three generations ago, when its last true
king, **Harrow III**, took a crown of unknown make from beneath the
castle rather than lose it to a rival claimant. The crown gave him a
reign with no natural end — and no substance either. He kept his throne.
The land around it hollowed out: crops failed, the court's faces grew
"thin," as the old accounts put it, and the castle itself began to sink,
floor by floor, into whatever the crown had been sealed above.

Ashveil's castle is now a vertical ruin, its throne room some six levels
underground, its old dungeons and cellars became the *upper* levels of
what people now call the **Hollow Crown** — the whole buried structure,
named for the artifact still resting, they say, on a king who never
managed to die.

The party is a group of outsiders — sellswords, a disgraced scholar of
the old court, an exile with a family claim, whoever the campaign's
opening roster turns out to be — hired or driven to descend and settle
what's down there, for reasons as mercenary or personal as we want each
character to be.

## Tone

Gothic, cold, quietly wrong rather than gory. Ashveil didn't fall to a
monster army — it fell to something more like a slow subtraction. The
horror is absence: rooms set for feasts no one is at, guards still
"posted" long after they stopped being able to leave. Combat encounters
(rot-things, the castle's former staff, things that used to be knights)
should read as *consequences* of the crown, not a generic monster
bestiary.

## Structure

The dungeon is organized in **descending acts**, each a handful of
levels, each corresponding to an era of Ashveil's decline as you go
deeper and further back in time:

1. **The Sunken Wards** — the original dungeons and cellars, now the
   uppermost levels; opportunistic squatters, collapsed masonry, the most
   "normal" of the ruin.
2. **The Long Court** — the castle's grand halls as they sank; the
   nobility's end, told through preserved (too preserved) banquet halls
   and galleries.
3. **The Still Garrison** — the castle guard, who never stood down;
   the game's main martial resistance and where combat systems get their
   heaviest workout.
4. **The Throne Beneath** — Harrow III himself, and the crown.

**v1 scope decision (Phase 6):** only Act 1, The Sunken Wards, ships —
4 levels, ending with Steward Marrow's defeat and the party's escape
back to the surface. Acts 2–4 above are the intended shape of the full
campaign and stay canon for a possible future expansion, but they are
not built and v1's ending (see
[08-roadmap-phases.md](08-roadmap-phases.md#phase-6--full-campaign--release-polish))
is written to close the story on its own terms at the end of Act 1, not
as a mid-campaign cliffhanger. Phases 0–4 used small placeholder/
greybox levels that didn't carry any of this narrative weight; Phase 5
is where Act 1 actually got its content pass.

## How story is delivered

In keeping with pillar 3 ("the dungeon is the character"):

- **Environmental storytelling first** — room composition, damage to the
  architecture, what's been moved or barricaded.
- **Lore items** — journal pages, letters, and inscriptions found while
  exploring, short (a paragraph or two), never mandatory reading.
- **Sparse NPC encounters** — a handful of non-hostile or
  conditionally-hostile figures per act, used for one memorable exchange
  each rather than branching dialogue trees.
- **No full cutscenes.** Big story beats happen in the first-person view
  itself (a door opening on a new room, a boss standing where you
  expected an empty hall), not in a cutaway.

## Open questions

Left open deliberately until later phases, so we don't lock in worldbuilding
that content production hasn't tested yet:

- Exact number/identity of the starting party members and whether any are
  fixed vs. player-created (see
  [03-party-and-characters.md](03-party-and-characters.md)).
- Whether the crown is ultimately destroyed, claimed, or something
  stranger — the ending is a Phase 5/6 decision.
- Whether other survivors of Ashveil exist outside the dungeon (relevant
  only if we ever add a surface hub).
